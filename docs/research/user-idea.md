# User's internship vision

Received October 15, 2026. Preserved from the user's message with whitespace normalized.
The narrow scope proposal does not replace this original vision.

An internship discovery and preparation planner for Indian undergraduates pursuing funded research and company internships abroad.
It helps students answer three questions: What kind of work suits me? Which opportunities should I target? How do I prepare before their deadlines?
The complete flow
1. Take a general “taste test”
Around 10–12 source-grounded questions introduce different work areas and suggest suitable directions. This is not a separate test for every internship, and it doesn’t lock students into a category.
2. Set preferences, like Scout
Students can edit their interests and filter by location, research/company opportunities, funding, remote work, visa needs, keywords and organizations.
3. Discover real opportunities using TinyFish
We search and read current sources—not just display a hardcoded list of programs. Students can filter, compare, save, hide and explicitly select internships. Results include source links, eligibility, deadlines and funding details; missing information stays marked unknown, rather than guessed.
4. Check eligibility and preparation
For selected targets, a short readiness interview assesses relevant skills against sourced requirements. Untested skills remain “Not assessed.” Documents and other requirements use checklists rather than artificial skill scores. Students also enter their weekly availability and exam periods.
5. Get grouped, personalized roadmaps
Targets are organized into Research and Company tracks. Compatible preparation—such as a shared skill or document—is planned once, while program-specific applications and selection stages remain separate.
6. Follow a realistic weekly plan
The overview highlights the next useful action, with a detailed timeline inside each track. Both tracks share one weekly time budget, and exam weeks are protected.
Important safeguards
- Shared work is scheduled before the earliest deadline it serves. If there isn’t enough time, the roadmap flags the target as at risk instead of pretending everything fits.
- Official deadlines, personal targets and unannounced future calls are treated differently. We cannot guarantee that a student meets every deadline or gets selected.
- Selection-process and interview preparation uses officially supported information, not invented stages or unverified anecdotes.
- “Funded” does not automatically mean zero expense; benefits, fees, exclusions and uncertainties remain visible.
